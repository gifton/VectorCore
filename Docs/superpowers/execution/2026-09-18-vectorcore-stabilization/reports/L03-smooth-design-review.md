# L03 smooth-distance design adjudication

**ACCEPT for this numerical subdesign, with the epsilon definition made explicit below.** This is a mathematical/source-domain review only, not acceptance of the whole L03 design or runtime evidence.

Let delta=max(0,Double(distance)-rho), z count zero deltas, q=k-z, and T=log2(k+1). Existing nonnegative finite Float distances and rho=min positive distance imply at least one zero delta in every nonempty row. Thus z>=1. When z<T, k>=2, q>0, and at least one positive delta exists. Keeping duplicate entries in k/z preserves the existing calibration semantics; later directed-duplicate max fusion is a separate operation.

For normalized positive deltas r in(0,1], F(tau)=z+sum exp(-r/tau) is continuous and increasing. At tau=2, F>=z+q*exp(-1/2)>=1+(k-1)*exp(-1/2). At k=2 the latter exceeds log2(3); the derivative of the difference for real k>=2 is exp(-1/2)-1/((k+1)*ln2)>0. The proposed upper endpoint therefore brackets T for every solvable row.

For the lower endpoint, let rmin=min positive r and L=-log(epsilon/q). Then tauLow=rmin/L gives exp(-r/tauLow)<=epsilon/q for every positive r, hence F(tauLow)<=z+epsilon. Define epsilon explicitly as positive finite, less than q, and preferably no greater than min(residualTolerance/4,(T-z)/2). The second condition gives a strict lower bracket rather than relying on 'small relative to tolerance' to imply one. If a tolerance-based epsilon alone is chosen and the endpoint is already within the residual tolerance, accept that endpoint explicitly; do not call F(low)<=T a proven invariant without checking it. Compute T as log2(Double(k)+1), avoiding an integer k+1 addition.

Bisection in log(tau) is appropriate. Across nonnegative finite Float distances, the smallest positive delta is bounded by the Float subnormal spacing, and the largest by Float.max. Their ratio is approximately at least2^-277, far above Double underflow. With a modest epsilon as specified, log endpoints, tau, physical sigma=scale*tau, and the exponent used in exp(-exp(log(r)-logtau)) remain within Double range. The upper physical bandwidth is at most2*Float.max; the lower nonzero bandwidth remains many orders above Double.min. Local/global mean floors also fit Double for any realizable collection of Float inputs. Using widened rho/sigma through membership avoids the current Float-narrowing failure.

Sixty-four log bisections are sufficient to reduce this finite log interval below ordinary Double resolution, but floating summation/stagnation still requires the explicit residual acceptance test. Test both endpoints/last candidate as appropriate and throw the selected typed failure if no finite result meets max(1e-5,1e-5*T). Exhaustion must not publish an unchecked value. No unconditional guarantee for arbitrary collection size or floating summation order is needed.

The z>=T policy is coherent: sigma0 is the chosen limiting pre-floor bandwidth, and existing positive local/global floor is subsequently applied. If positive deltas exist, sigma0 gives weight0 for those entries and weight1 for zero deltas; if there are no positive deltas, all memberships are1 independently of bandwidth. This handles singleton, pure duplicate, tied-minimum and equality cases without claiming a finite positive root. Isolated rows remain rho=sigma0 with no memberships. A floor can change the defining-equation residual, so only the pre-floor solve carries that residual guarantee.

Multiplicative scale behavior follows from invariant normalized deltas and a homogeneously scaling floor/global mean, subject to the stated Float input/output and Double arithmetic rounding. This does not imply an SGD layout-scale guarantee. Tests should separate solvable closed-form [s,2s], floor-dominated solvable rows, infeasible tied rows, equality/singleton rows, pure duplicates, isolated rows, and duplicate-ID fusion. Test zero-bandwidth positive-delta membership directly even if an ordinary positive floor makes that branch uncommon in complete graph construction.
